<h2><span class="num">02</span>Where the claim came from</h2>

In August, a Floodlight and Guardian drone investigation found dozens of gas generators running at the DataOne data center in Vineland, New Jersey. On September 22 the state fined the company $1.07 million, after an inspection on July 29 found 62 large gas generators installed and operating without permits.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> On October 2, Floodlight reported an expert estimate of how much pollution those generators put out.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> NJ.com's video report, the headline here, builds on that reporting. We could not open the NJ.com page, so we rate the headline as it was sent to us and rely on Floodlight's reporting for the facts.

<h2><span class="num">03</span>What the evidence actually shows</h2>

<Stat
  big="~140 tons of NOx"
  desc="Rutgers professor Xiaomeng Jin's best estimate of what the 62 generators emitted over nearly a year. It would rank DataOne in the state's top five NOx polluters of 2025."

NOx is a group of smog-forming gases linked to asthma and heart attacks. Floodlight sent generator specifications it got through records requests to two experts and asked them to work out the emissions.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> The generators arrived in July 2025, ran in stages starting in October 2025, and were all running by June. DataOne's own letter to the state says they ran more than 46,000 hours in total.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

<Stat

The state fined the company and ordered it to get permits or stop. It gave DataOne 45 days and let the generators keep running in that time.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup> DataOne says it disagrees with the state's finding, but will apply for permits and is moving to fuel cells.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

<h2><span class="num">05</span>What the headline gets right</h2>

The facts under it are solid. The state itself says 62 large generators ran without required permits, and fined the company a record amount for it.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> The size of the pollution is also large by any measure used in the reporting: 140 tons of NOx would put one data center among New Jersey's top five NOx sources of 2025, and the site's potential emissions are far above the 25 tons a year at which New Jersey calls a facility a "major source."<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Whether "staggering" is the right word is a judgment, but the numbers support a strong word.

<h2><span class="num">06</span>What it gets wrong or leaves out</h2>

**The 140 tons is an estimate.** It comes from generator specifications and run hours, not from air monitors at the site. The article does not say what assumptions the experts used. It is a careful estimate by a named expert, but it is not a measurement.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

**"Is emitting" may be out of date.** The state gave DataOne 45 days from late September to get permits or stop.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup> That window has now passed. We found no report of a permit being issued or of the generators shutting down, so we cannot say whether the pollution is continuing. DataOne says the generators are temporary, until fuel cells take over.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

**The worst numbers are potential, not actual.** The 1,500-ton figure is what the fleet could emit at full use, not what it did emit.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

<h2><span class="num">07</span>Where the real concern is</h2>

The larger concern is how this was allowed. The generators arrived in July 2025, and local officials noted them in early 2026, but the state did not find them until July 2026. Residents had filed more than 100 noise complaints by then.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Environmental groups say a $1 million fine is small for a company this size.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

<h2><span class="num">08</span>What we're not sure about</h2>

We could not read the NJ.com page, and the word "staggering" does not appear in any source we read in full, so we cannot say who first used it. We have not seen the Rutgers analysis itself, only Floodlight's account of it, so we could not check its assumptions. We do not know whether the generators are still running or whether DataOne has applied for permits. DataOne has not responded to the emissions estimate.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> We did not find a measured emissions figure for the site.
